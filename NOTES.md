# Dintlha tsa phetolelo — Translation Notes

*Faele ena e bontsha ditemana tseo letsoho la motho kapa qeto e di amileng — hore
mobadi a kgone ho bona hore na temana e ngotswe ke mochini feela, e lokisitswe, kapa
e laolwa ke qeto, le hore na hobaneng. Dintlha tse ding di ngotswe ka Senyesemane,
hobane di bua ka sebopeho sa mantswe a Seheberu; ke puo ya mosebetsi e
arolelanwang ke diphetolelo tsohle tsa Selah. Potso e kgolo e bulehileng ke ya
**mengolo e mmedi** — Afrika Borwa le Lesotho — mme e emetse mobadi wa Sesotho.*

This file records every verse where a human hand or a ruling touched the Sesotho
rendering, and the questions that wait for a Sesotho reader. Rules: the Selah
Sesotho discipline (`st.md`) — **Yahwe** wherever the Name stands; `⟨את⟩` kept
wherever the Hebrew has את; `⟨…⟩` marks only words the Hebrew did not write; Hebrew
sound for personal and place names. For how the text was made, and the counts, see
`PROVENANCE.md`.

---

## Genesis 1:1

```
Qalong Elohim o ile a bopa ⟨את⟩ mahodimo le ⟨את⟩ lefatshe.
```

Both markers in place, `o ile a bopa` for ברא, `Elohim` at אלהים, and the South
African `lefatshe` (not Lesotho *lefatše*). This is the form the rules ask for.

## Deuteronomy 6:4

```
Utloa, Yisraele: Yahwe Elohim ya rona — Yahwe o le mong.
```

The Name twice, unchanged; אלהינו → `Elohim ya rona` keeps the plural noun with
the first-person-plural suffix rendered as a possessive; אחד → `o le mong`. But
`Utloa` (שמע) is the **Lesotho** spelling — South African orthography writes
*Utlwa*. Left as it stands; see the open question below.

## Psalm 23:1 — a known fault, waiting to be re-rendered

```
Psalme ya Dawide: Yahwe ⟨⟨ke⟩⟩ modisa wa ka; nke ke seke ya ⟨את⟩ ntho.
```

Two faults. (1) אחסר (qal imperfect 1cs, *I shall lack*) carries no את, yet its
gloss reads `ke seke ya ⟨את⟩ ntho` — a marker on a token whose Hebrew has none.
(2) The supplied copula is wrapped twice, `⟨⟨ke⟩⟩`. The Name and רעי → `modisa wa
ka` are sound. Recorded here rather than silently fixed.

## Isaiah 53:5 — two orthographies in one verse

```
Empa o ile a boretswa ka ditlolo tsa rona, a thotsoa ka ditsholohetso tsa rona;
thibelo ya khotso ya rona e ne e le hodima hae, mme ka mehlabano ya hae re fola.
```

מחלל (pual participle, *pierced / profaned*) → `a boretswa` uses the South African
`-tswa`; מדכא (pual participle, *crushed*) → `a thotsoa` uses the Lesotho `-tsoa`.
Same verse, same passive pattern, two spellings. Also for a Sesotho ear: מוסר
(*discipline, chastisement*) came out as `thibelo` — is that the right word here?

## The orthography ruling (2026-10-03)

The rules write South African Sesotho, but the first hour showed a minority of
verses in Lesotho spelling (`tš`, `-oa`: *tšoara*, *lefatše*, *ntša*), and a
smaller set carrying **Northern Sotho (Sepedi)** words written with `š` (*mošomi*
worker, *bošupa* seven, *mošemane* boy, *dimpša* dogs) — those are another
language's words, not a spelling variant. Ruling: **one orthography, South
African.** The affected verses were re-rendered. Many came back in Lesotho spelling
again — a general instruction did not hold against a spelling the model had
learned — so the South African forms were named one by one in the rules (`tshepe`,
`tshaba`, `ntsha`, `lefatshe`, `tshwara`, `tshwana`; `mollo`, `me`, `mona`,
`monga`; `Koreshe`, `Kheshbone`), and the remainder were corrected by spelling only
(`tš → tsh`, `ô → o`, `ê → e`), in the sentence and the word rows together.

Four words with `š` were left, because they are words, not spellings, and need an
ear: `šireletsa`, `šumella`, `mašiša`, `sekošo`.

Lesotho forms still stand in places — `Utloa` (Deut 6:4), `o tšabe` (Deut 10:12),
`a thotsoa` (Isa 53:5).

## `⟨את⟩` after the orthography re-render

Re-rendered verses were compared with the text before, for the marker. Against the
**word rows** (the Hebrew is the truth, not the previous sentence), some apparent
losses were the removal of a surplus marker and were kept; the real losses were
restored from the earlier text with the spelling correction applied.

## `ngaka` — kept, lawful (per-token verdict)

The rules forbid `senohe` (the diviner, Deut 18) and `ngaka` as renderings of נביא.
Two verses carry `ngaka`, and both are lawful:

- **Leviticus 20:27** — the verse is itself about the medium (אוב): the word names
  what the Hebrew names.
- **Jeremiah 8:22** — הצרי אין בגלעד אם רפא אין שם, *is there no physician there?*
  רפא is a healer; `ngaka` is the Sesotho word for a doctor.

## Set aside for review

- **Deuteronomy 10:12, Judges 2:1, Genesis 24:32** — the word rows and the sentence
  do not say the same thing.
- **Numbers 18:26** — on the same list.
- **Proverbs 3:33** — the sentence garbled and the word rows lacked the Name token;
  removed and being re-rendered.

## Being re-rendered now

English words had leaked **inside the supplied-word brackets** (`⟨the⟩` and the
like) — the English sample words of the instructions copied into the place meant
for Sesotho. The same leak was found across several Selah renderings. The affected
verses, together with verses whose word rows did not match the Hebrew token count
and verses left empty, were removed and are being re-rendered now. Until that
finishes, some verses are absent from this repository. No final tally is given
here.

## Open questions for a Sesotho reader

1. **Which orthography?** The rules chose South African (`tsh`, `w`), and Lesotho
   spelling (`tš`, `oa`) has been treated as a variant to remove. A Lesotho reader
   may see it the other way round. Should the rendering keep one, keep both, or
   follow the reader?
2. **`Molimo` or `Modimo`?** The rules write the god-word with `l` before `i` — the
   Lesotho form — inside a South African orthography that writes `Modimo`. It never
   stands in place of the Name either way; lowercase `molimo` / `melimo` is used for
   the gods of the nations (Exod 32:8). Which spelling does a South African reader
   expect?
3. **`Elohim` or `Elohime`?** — a consonant-final transliteration, or one closed
   with a vowel like the personal names (Abrahame, Yitshake)?
4. **`kh` for ח** (`ruakh`, `Mashiakh`, `minkha`) — or `h`?
5. **`moporofeta` for נביא** — or a transliteration (*nabi*)?
6. **Hebrew-sound names** (Abrahame · Yitshake · Yaakobo · Dawide · Yisraele ·
   Mitsrayime · Yerushalayime) against the names a Sesotho Bible reader knows.
7. **`Torah`** with final `h`, or `Tora`?
8. **The four `š` words** above — Sesotho, or another language?
